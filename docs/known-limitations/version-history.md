# Version history

Saving, comparing and applying versions. Part of [Known limitations](../known-limitations.md).

See [Version history](../versions.md) for how versions work.

- **Applying a version usually makes earlier AI edits impossible to revert.** A write in a note's **AI edit history** can be reverted only while the blocks it changed are exactly as it left them. Applying a version replaces the whole note, so that is usually no longer the case.
- **Ctrl+Z can't undo applying a version.** The applied content comes from the server, like an AI write, and Ctrl+Z only undoes your own edits. To go back, apply another version.
- **Applying a version overwrites changes that nobody has saved as a version, including other people's.** The dialog that appears when there are such changes says so, and **Apply without saving** discards them. If someone edits the note while the apply is under way, the apply can stop without changing anything, and the dialog appears again.
- **Everyone who edits a note between two versions is listed on the later one, without saying who changed what.** People and AI assistants whose changes fall within the same quiet stretch end up in one version — including an AI write and the edits made around it.
- **Comparing versions of a very large note takes a moment.** The comparison is worked out in your browser. On a note of 2,000 paragraphs, with the version history panel open, the time from clicking the note's first version to the first change marker appearing in the side-by-side comparison against the current state was 806, 787 and 771 ms in three runs (median 787 ms; headless Chromium under WSL2, a 1400×900 window, the end-to-end test stack), with the two columns already lined up block by block. Without a browser (jsdom), decoding the versions of such a note took a median of 53 ms and working out the comparison 11 ms. On the server, saving a version of a 2,000-block note by hand took 54, 70 and 65 ms, and fetching it about 4 ms (a 393,083-byte response), in an integration test against a local PostgreSQL 17.
- **Versions don't count toward storage limits, and there is no site-wide cap on them.** The retention settings in **Site admin → Storage plans** are the only control, and they thin out automatic versions only: manual versions stay until someone deletes them, the note is permanently deleted, or its history is cleared. Their size is shown as **Current backups** under **Version history** in **Settings → Storage** and on a group's **Storage** page, and, together with the size of note content, as **Notes and history** in **Site admin → Users** and **Groups**.
- **A block moved under a different parent block shows as deleted in one place and added in another.** Blocks are matched only among the same parent's blocks, and only blocks that change order there are marked as moved. Among the same parent's blocks, a block that the editor gave a new identity is still matched when its content is exactly the same — so text you push down by pressing Enter at the start of its paragraph isn't shown as deleted and added, as long as you don't also change it. Pasting over a whole note gives every block a new identity: blocks whose content is exactly the same are matched wherever they are, and a text block (a paragraph, heading, list item, checklist item or quote) of at least four words — or four characters, in Chinese, Japanese or Korean — whose wording stayed close is matched too, showing what changed inside it. Close wording is looked for only within about 30 blocks of where the previous such match was found, so after a run of more than 30 earlier blocks that were removed or rewritten beyond recognition, the rest of that list falls back to deleted and added. Other blocks, such as images, tables and code, and shorter text blocks are matched by content only when they are exactly the same. Empty paragraphs are matched with each other too, but are never marked as moved.
- **Side by side, the columns line up only block by block, at the top level.** Each row is as tall as the taller of its two blocks, so the shorter side leaves blank space below its block. Blocks marked as moved aren't lined up with their counterpart: each keeps its place in its own column, opposite a striped gap or whatever else isn't lined up there. The same goes for an empty paragraph matched with an empty paragraph elsewhere in the other version, when lining them up would cross other matched blocks. Blocks nested inside another block, such as sub-items of a list, aren't lined up one by one — only the top-level block they belong to is. When an image or other embedded content finishes loading after the comparison appears and makes a block taller, the columns are out of line for a moment until they are lined up again.
- **In the side-by-side comparison, a row of side-by-side columns lines up as one unit.** The columns inside it are not aligned one by one; each side shows the row as it is in its own version. In either comparison, columns are matched by their identity, not their position: when two columns swap places, one of them is marked as moved, and deleting a column and adding another one shows one deleted and one added column — in the single-column comparison, which shows both versions merged, the row then has more columns than either version. A block moved from one column to another shows as deleted in one and added in the other.
- **A row of side-by-side columns is marked a little differently from other blocks in a comparison.** A column whose width changed is marked as changed, with no **Changed · see before and after** button. Every column in a comparison gets 8 pixels of extra space on its left, where its marker goes. A column that was added or deleted is marked along with each block inside it, so those show two coloured bars next to each other.
- **Copying a note doesn't bring its versions along, moving a note into a group deletes its versions and comments unless you choose to bring them along, and transferring a group's notes deletes their versions.** There is also no "before" version for older notes: the first time a note that existed before version history was added is edited, the content it had until then isn't kept as a version.
- **Versions kept through a move into a group show who edited them to the group's editors.** Each kept version still shows its name and its editors' handles, profile pictures and agent labels. These can belong to people the note was shared with before the move who aren't in the group. Moving without checking **Bring its version history and comments along** clears the history and the comments instead.
- **Changes that were still waiting to be saved when a note is moved into a group with its history kept go into the group's next version, and their editors aren't listed on it.** The move clears the waiting state, so the next version that is saved contains those changes but lists only the people who edited after the move (and, for a version saved by hand, who saved it).
- **Applying a version to a note whose content is malformed fails with a server error.** This affects a note whose blocks are missing their ids, or are nested thousands of levels deep; AI writes to such a note fail the same way, as they did before version history.
- **Deleting a group and transferring its notes deletes all of their versions in the same database transaction.** In an integration test against a local PostgreSQL 17, transferring 50 notes with 3 versions each took 81, 83 and 92 ms in three runs (median 83 ms).
- **Changing an Automatic versions switch reaches a note that is open right now only after everyone has closed it.** This applies to all three switches (the site's, a personal space's and a group's).
- **If automatic versions are off for a note when its waiting changes come due** (X minutes after the last change, set in **Site admin → Storage plans**, 5 by default), **those changes stop waiting.** For a note that is open at that moment, what counts is how the switches were set when it was opened (or last moved); otherwise, how they are set at that moment — so for a note nobody has open, turning a switch off and on again before then doesn't make them stop waiting. Once the changes have stopped waiting, no automatic version is saved for them on their own, even after automatic versions are turned on again, and the people who made them aren't listed on a later version. The changes stay in the note, and a version saved later — by hand, or automatically after further edits — contains them.
- **When a version is applied at almost the same moment as someone else types, those few characters may not count as a change until the note is edited again**, so no automatic version is saved for them on their own until then.
- **If the server can't read a note's version settings when the note is opened, saving a version by hand answers that the server is busy until everyone has closed the note, and changes made in the meantime aren't, on their own, saved as an automatic version until the note is edited again after it is reopened.** Changes that were already waiting to be saved stay waiting: they are saved once the X minutes are up, but not before everyone has closed the note. Separately, changes saved in the moment right after a note is moved into a group or transferred, while its version tracking is being set up again, aren't counted as a change until the note is edited again — whether or not anyone leaves the note.
- **Deleting a version at the moment someone applies it leaves the note without a base version.** The content is still applied, but **Current state** reads **No base version**. No version is saved straight away; with automatic versions on, the next change to its content leads to an automatic version once the note goes quiet.
- **Deleting a version at the moment someone else's apply of that version finishes can leave the note based on a version that no longer exists.** **Current state** then shows that version's made-up name and code followed by **(deleted)**, until a new version is saved or another version is applied.
- **An automatic version appears a little after the X minutes are up.** Changes are stored on the server about 2 seconds after the last keystroke (at least every 10 seconds while someone keeps typing; an AI or API write is stored at once), and the server looks for notes due for a version every 30 seconds, so a version normally appears within about half a minute after the X minutes are up. When more than 100 notes are due at once (for example right after a restart), each further 100 wait one more 30-second round, and a check still running when the next one is due makes that one skip a round.
- **If the server fails to record a change and the note isn't changed again, that change isn't saved as an automatic version.** Saving a version by hand still works.
- **Someone whose edit is saved at the very moment an automatic version is being made may be listed on the wrong version.** If the note is open, they are listed both on that version and on the next one (the next one only if the note changes again); if it isn't open, they aren't listed on that version, and are listed on the next one only if there is still something new to save when it comes due (for example because the note changed again). This affects only the list of names, not the content.
- **A note that keeps failing to be saved as an automatic version is retried every 10 minutes, with at most one warning in the server log per try.** A note due to be saved whose version settings couldn't be read when it was opened is checked again every 30 seconds, without further warnings, until everyone has closed it.
- **Saving a note's content also works out a fingerprint of the note, to tell whether the content really changed.** On a note of 2,000 paragraphs this took 3.1 ms (median of 21 runs; Node 24.14.0 on Windows, outside the test stack).
- **Made-up version names repeat.** There are 4,096 of them, so two versions of a note can share one; the short code is what tells them apart. The word lists are fixed for good: changing them would rename existing versions.
- **When the version that another version is based on has been deleted, the deleted version's own name isn't shown**: it appears with its made-up name and code followed by **(deleted)**. A base that had already been deleted before this release started recording the ids of bases shows as **a deleted version**, and so can some bases after a rollback to 0.10 (see the next item).
- **After rolling back to 0.10 and upgrading again, some bases are shown wrongly.** 0.10 doesn't record the id of the version a note or a version is based on. For a note whose base changed while on 0.10 — a version was saved or applied there — **Current state** can name the base the note had when the site was rolled back, or read **Based on a deleted version**, until a new version is saved or a version is applied after upgrading again. A version saved on 0.10 on top of an older version shows as based on **a deleted version** for good.
- **If saving the applied content, or clearing the waiting changes, fails while a version is being applied**, the changes made before the apply stay waiting, and the applied content may later be saved as an automatic version listing the people who made those earlier changes.
- **Moving a note into a group without keeping its history, or deleting a group and transferring its notes, can fail with "The server is busy" while the background cleanup of old versions is working on those notes.** Try again.
- **While a notice is showing at the bottom of the screen (for about 5 seconds), the first Esc closes the notice rather than the dialog that is open.** Press Esc again to close the dialog. This applies throughout the app.
- **Pressing Esc in the sidebar's search box while previewing a version also closes the preview**, besides clearing the search.
- **Applying a version from before the upgrade to 0.11 brings back ordinary links, and the one-time conversion doesn't convert them again.** That includes the version named `連結轉換前` that the one-time conversion saves. This is deliberate: it is the way to undo the conversion for one note. Links you paste, drag in, type or write afterwards are converted as usual (#269).
- **In a comparison or the AI box's preview, a link to a note you can't see can show as removed and added again inside a block that changed for another reason.** Such a link with no text of its own is shown with the text it was made with, and the two versions can hold different such text. A block where only the text a link was made with differs isn't marked as changed (#269).
- **On a short window, such as a phone held sideways, the floating version history and comments panels are short too.** From 640 to 1279 pixels wide the panel ends above the round buttons at the bottom right so they stay in reach. When that would leave it too short, only the arrow button stays while the panel is open; at 844×390 the panel is then still only about 177 pixels tall, and its list scrolls inside it.
